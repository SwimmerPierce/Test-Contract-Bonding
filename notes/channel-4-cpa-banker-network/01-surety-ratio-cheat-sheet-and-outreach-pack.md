#### B. Key Balance Sheet Ratios & Rules of Thumb
1. **Working Capital (`Current Assets - Current Liabilities`):**
   - Industry rule of thumb: Sureties commonly look for adjusted Working Capital equal to **~10%+ of total uncompleted bonded backlog** (varying by trade, facility tier, and asset liquidity).
   - *Common pitfalls that shrink surety Working Capital:* large shareholder advances/receivables (`due from related parties` are typically **deducted 100%** by surety underwriters), obsolete inventory, and uncollectible A/R >90 days.
2. **Tangible Net Worth / Equity:**
   - Typically benchmarked alongside Working Capital (`~10%+` of backlog). Retaining earnings inside the operating corp rather than stripping all cash via year-end dividends dramatically expands bonding capacity.
3. **Debt-to-Equity Ratio:**
   - Underwriters prefer total liabilities to tangible net worth under **`2.0x – 3.0x`** (higher leverage signals tight cash buffers during project delays).
4. **Work-in-Progress (WIP) Schedule Must-Haves:**
   - Every open project listed with: Contract Price, Costs Incurred to Date, Estimated Cost to Complete, Billings to Date, Over/Under-Billings, and Gross Profit Margin %.
   - Consistent profit fade (`margin erosion` from bid to completion) reduces underwriting confidence faster than almost any other metric.
5. **Bank Line of Credit Synergy (For Commercial Bankers):**
   - **Standby Bid/Performance Bonds do NOT tie up bank operating lines**—unlike Bank Letters of Credit (`LOCs`), which consume 100% of borrowing capacity dollar-for-dollar.
   - Referring developers and contractors to replace municipal LOCs with Subdivision/Servicing Surety Bonds frees up their bank line for working capital and equipment financing.
